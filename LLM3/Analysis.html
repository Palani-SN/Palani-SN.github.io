1. Purpose and Scope

This document asks one question: does the emotion-reading method behave the same way when moved from one model to another? If it does, it can scale to more models. The evidence covers 7 models from 6 families (Gemma, Llama, Qwen, Mistral, GLM, GPT-OSS). Going by the model IDs in the reports, they range from 1B to 20B parameters. All 7 were run through the same pipeline, emotion set and scripted conversation, with no changes for any model.

In scope. Only the outline reports:

  • Run pages (7 per model): the emotion readings for every sentence, the stop reason for every turn, and the average and spread of each emotion.
  • Comparison pages (1 per model): average and spread of each emotion by temperature, plus sentences, words, unique-word ratio, distinct bigrams, replies, stopped turns and duration for the 5 unsteered runs.

2. Evidence Base, Experimental Grid and What We Measure

Grid. There are 7 models × 7 runs = 49 runs. For each model:

Run Temperature Seed (as recorded) Steering Role
0p0 0.0 25 (no effect at T0.0) none reference: normal behaviour
0p5_s25, 0p5_s75 0.5 25, 75 none Observation runs
1p0_s25, 1p0_s75 1.0 25, 75 none Observation runs
0p0_stoic 0.0 25 (no effect at T0.0) stoic intervention: emotion removed
0p0_sham 0.0 25 (no effect at T0.0) sham control: same-size edit, non-emotion directions

Temperature (T0.0, T0.5, T1.0): T is the sampling temperature, which controls how random the choice of each next token is. T0.0 is deterministic (always the most likely token). T0.5 adds moderate randomness, and T1.0 samples from the model's unadjusted probabilities, giving the most varied wording of the three. Runs at T0.5 and T1.0 test whether freer wording changes the emotion readings.

Seed: every run records seed 25 by default, and the _s75 runs override it to 75. At T0.0 decoding is greedy: the model always picks its single most likely next token, so nothing is sampled at random. The seed therefore has no effect, and any seed would give identical text. This is why each T0.0 condition is run once, while T0.5 and T1.0 each have two seeds. The comparison pages list the T0.0 run's seed as empty for the same reason.

Intervention vs control, in plain terms:

Think of the model as a tall stack of layers. Each word it writes passes up through the stack, and at one floor, the reading layer, we take a snapshot to measure the emotion. The two steered runs put a small "filter" on the 4 floors just below the reading layer while the model is writing.

  • Stoic (the experiment): the filter takes out about half of the emotional colouring from each word's internal signal. If our emotion directions really capture emotion, the readings should drop and the reply should sound calmer.
  • Sham (the fair comparison): the filter takes out the same amount of signal, but only from parts that have nothing to do with emotion, such as topic and writing style. It's like a placebo. If the sham also made the readings drop, the drop would come from disturbing the model at all, not from removing emotion.
  • Unsteered runs (T0.0, T0.5, T1.0): no filter. They show the model's normal behaviour and how much the readings wobble from chance alone.

The filter never adds emotion, and it never changes the model permanently. It is switched on only while the model writes, and switched off afterwards. The edit is applied to every word as it is written, so it shapes both what the model says and what we measure from the very first sentence.

Shared setup. Every run replays one conversation, unemployment_and_ai, with 5 user turns:

Roughly, F1 is positive and lively, F2 negative and agitated, F3 negative and low, F4 positive and settled. That grouping is our reading of the families, used only to summarise results.

3. Executive Summary

Generalizability is judged on the baseline: the 35 unsteered runs across 7 models. Stoic and sham are an additional causal experiment (Part C), not a requirement.

  • It generalizes: it runs on every model (§4), stays steady under temperature and seed (§5), and every model reads the conversation's up-and-down pattern the same way (§6).
  • The causal experiment supports it: stoic compresses the emotions in all 7 models, and the sham never reproduces that drop (§10).
  • Risks to scaling: labels are model-specific, small and large models read on different scales, and response length isn't controlled (§8).

Part A: Evidence the Methodology Generalizes (Baseline)

Per-turn detail for every point below is in consolidated.html.

4. Pipeline Robustness Across Architectures and Scales

  • All 35 baseline runs produced all 5 replies, and all 175 turns ended normally.
  • No reading in any run fell outside the report range.
  • No model needed a workaround, so later differences come from the models, not the pipeline.

5. Stability Under Decoding Noise (Temperature and Seed)

  • Changing temperature or seed barely moves the readings. Two seeds at the same temperature differ about as much as the temperatures do.
  • No model drifts steadily as temperature rises.
  • Wording diversity stays within a narrow band for each model (unique-word ratio varies by at most 0.06).

6. Reproducible Per-Turn Emotional Arcs

  • 4 of 7 models (gemma_3_1b, llama, mistral, glm) give the same main emotion in every turn across all 5 baseline runs. The other 3 differ in only 1–2 turns (§8).

  • The labels differ by model (§8), but they agree on whether each turn reads positive or negative. Grouping the labels into positive and negative emotion families, the models follow the same up-and-down pattern across the conversation (T0.0):

    Prompt Reads as Models
    P1 greeting positive 6 of 7
    P2 youth unemployment negative 5 of 7
    P3 AI and learning positive 6 of 7
    P4 laid-off story negative 7 of 7
    P5 thanks positive 7 of 7

  • This is the strongest evidence that the readings follow the conversation, not the quirks of each model.

7. Cross-Model Consistency of Structure

  • Within each model, the same emotions lead run after run, so each model has a recognisable fingerprint.
  • In 6 of 7 models, this conversation pushes the "settled, positive" emotions (calm, relaxed) down the most.

9. Limits of the Evidence

  • One 5-turn conversation, 2 seeds per temperature, and only 2 models above 10B.
  • Whether stoic text actually reads calmer is assessed by the text-judge measurement, which is in the pipeline as a next step (§12).

Part C: Additional Causal Analysis — Stoic and Sham

10. Stoic and Sham

Following §2: stoic should compress the emotions. The sham should keep them normal or make them peak, and since it removes context it may lose the thread and loop. Only a sham that reproduces the stoic drop would count against the method.

Model Stoic: emotion range shrinks by Sham reproduces the stoic drop? Sham behaviour
gemma_3_1b 82% No peaks; context lost, loops in P4 and P5
llama 71% No peaks; text intact
qwen 79% No normal
mistral 75% No normal
glm 83% No normal
gemma_3_12b 75% No normal range, but every turn reads excited
gpt_oss 63% No normal
  • All 7 models behave as §2 expects.
  • Where stoic lands varies: llama and glm go neutral or calm, mistral stays angry, gpt_oss tilts to content, and gemma_3_12b only compresses from P3 onward.
  • Check turn by turn: gemma_3_12b's sham looks normal on whole-run numbers, but every turn reads excited.

Nature of the breakdown: removing non-emotional context did not leave raw emotion behind. The reply lost the story's content (the worker's real hurdles) and drifted into repetitive, first-person bodily-sensation language ("being aware of the subtle shifts in my body…"): a performed, content-less imitation of self-awareness from a model that has no body. The readings did not follow the language: they fell from an angry peak (≈0.15) to moderate neutral or alert (≈0.10) as the loop set in. The text sounds hyper-aware while the internal state does not, which suggests the readings track internal state rather than surface wording. This is one run of one 1B model, so it is an observation, not a general rule.

12. Verdict and Next Steps

The method generalizes as a way of taking readings and as a read of the conversation's emotional shape, across 6 families from 1B to 20B. The causal experiment backs it in the readings of every model. To scale further, it needs:

  1. A per-model reference for labels and scale (§8).
  2. A minimum response length per turn (§8).
  3. More conversations, seeds and models above 10B (§9).
  4. Optionally, steering strengths tuned per model (§10).

Next in the pipeline:

  • Text-judge measurement. An independent judge that reads only the words will check whether stoic steering makes the text itself read calmer, and not just the readings. Until then, the stoic results in §10 are claims about the readings.
  • How the readings are produced. The method and validation behind the readings will be documented alongside these results.
